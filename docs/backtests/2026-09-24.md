# Take-profit rules vs the live rulebook (2026-09-24)

**Question (user):** "the rulebook is to TAKE-PROFIT, maximizing it at all time."
**Finding:** every take-profit rule tested made less money than holding winners with
protective stops. The cheapest one still gave up a quarter of the growth; fixed
targets gave up half or more. The only thing take-profit buys is a shallower worst
drawdown, and only the partial version buys enough of it to matter for the floor.

## Setup
Same engine and costs as `2026-09-21.md` (14 DR-eligible names in THB, 0.2215%/side +
35 bps spread, decide on close, fill next open). Baseline = the real live rulebook:
quarterly top-3 (126 d momentum, SMA200 filter) + hard stop -20% from entry + trend
break (close < SMA200). Sleeve 55% momentum, 25% TDEX, 20% cash.
Variants add one exit layer. "cash" = proceeds idle until the next quarterly re-pick;
"rotate" = proceeds go straight into the next-ranked name. Trailing = profit-lock:
fires only while the position is above entry and has fallen X% from its highest close.

The 3-phase edge of "trailing 25%, rotate" over baseline was start-date luck; over 9
phases it trails in every column that matters. Baseline's worst drawdown (26.6%) from
today's ฿207k lands at ฿152k, through the ฿160k and ฿155k breaker lines; "sell half at
+30%" is the only variant whose worst case (17.4%, ฿171k) clears the whole ladder.

## Why take-profit loses here
Momentum returns are a few large winners carrying many small losers. A fixed target
sells exactly those winners early; the re-deployed cash goes into a weaker name and
pays two more sets of fees and spread. The hard stop and trend break already remove
the losers; what is left after a take-profit layer is a strategy with the losers'
downside and a capped upside.
